A Report Name Is Only the Starting Point

Current-owner, two-owner and full-search labels can conceal different scopes. Specify the property and parcel, relevant owner names, requested period and record categories. Agree the search depth with your legal or title team before ordering.

Match the Request to the Deliverable

  • Legal and vesting deed report: recorded deed reference, owner names, vesting wording and legal description.
  • Open-mortgage research: located mortgage instruments and requested related assignments, modifications or releases.
  • Current-owner / O&E research: agreed ownership period, encumbrance categories and copies.
  • Two-owner or defined-depth research: the specified owners, years or chain, with included record categories stated.
  • Tax, municipal and zoning research: separately identified offices and components.

Recorded Documents and Other Sources

Flagler County’s official-records guidance, for example, lists deeds, mortgages, assignments, satisfactions, plats, liens and other recorded instruments. That list describes a record source. It does not make each item part of every report or replace separate departmental and tax inquiries.

A Useful Request

“For [address/parcel] in [county/state], please quote [report type] covering [owners/period], including [record categories and copies]. Separately identify [tax/municipal components] and any unavailable records. Our matter reference is [reference] and the requested date is [date].”

Keep the Result and the Opinion Distinct

The report records factual findings within the agreed sources and scope. It does not insure title, establish loan ownership or decide the legal effect of an instrument. Refer questions about depth, gaps or enforceability to the reviewing professional.
