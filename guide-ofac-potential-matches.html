REQUEST-PREPARATION GUIDE · 10 NOVEMBER 2026

Start Here

An OFAC search can return similar names. Record the search scope and available identifiers so the client’s compliance team can assess potential matches.

Define the Names Before the Search

List each individual and entity separately. Provide exact names, known aliases, relevant countries and identifying details already available for the transaction. Confirm which lists are in scope and what search documentation your reviewer requires.

Understand a Potential Match

OFAC’s tool uses approximate name matching against its SDN and consolidated non-SDN lists. Similar names can return results for different parties. OFAC’s match-assessment guidance calls for comparison with available identifying details and escalation under the organization’s compliance procedures.

Keep a Useful Search Record

  • Subject names and aliases submitted.
  • Agreed lists, source, search date and available search settings.
  • Result entries and relevant identifying details.
  • Supporting search evidence, unresolved questions and the reviewing contact.

A Useful Request

“Please document an OFAC list search for [names and aliases], with [identifying details available]. Confirm the lists in scope and include the date, search evidence and potential matches. Send identity questions to [reviewing contact] before treating a result as resolved.”

Keep Screening Separate from Transaction Approval

Name screening is one input to a broader sanctions assessment. Ownership and geographic restrictions can require additional review. A result with no matching name does not authorize a transaction; the client’s legal or compliance team determines the outcome.
