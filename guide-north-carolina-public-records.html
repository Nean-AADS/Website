North Carolina Public-Record Research: UCC, Deeds and Court Records

Define Secretary of State UCC work, county land-record requests and court-record retrieval separately, with the exact names, sources, dates and certifications your matter needs.

North Carolina’s Secretary of State distinguishes free UCC research from an office search requested on UCC-11. Its UCC section maintains UCC and federal lien documents; deed and court requests require separate source instructions.

Current official UCC guidance · Source checked 11 November 2026.

STATE REQUEST-PREPARATION GUIDE · 10 NOVEMBER 2026

Start with the Source Map

Use this guide to prepare the request. It maps source types and questions to confirm; it is not a statewide completeness certificate or a legal determination of required scope.

Specify the UCC Deliverable

The Secretary of State maintains a UCC section and publishes its forms, including a UCC-11 information request. An interactive lookup, requested official response and copies of specified filings should not be treated as interchangeable. Identify the required deliverable and search through date.

Separate Deeds from Court Records

North Carolina’s land-record guidance identifies both county Register of Deeds offices and county Clerks of Superior Court as sources for different records affecting land. A deed copy or a single index search does not answer every property or litigation question.

Use Court Search Results as a Starting Point

The Judicial Branch explains access to case information and how to request court files and copies from the clerk. Identify the court, case number and particular documents needed. Online access and a docket listing are different from obtaining an official or certified record.

Request Checklist

  • Exact debtor or entity names, requested UCC search response and copy requirements.
  • County, property address, parcel identifier, owners and defined search depth.
  • Requested Register of Deeds records and any court-source components.
  • Court, case categories, period and specified documents or certifications.
  • Separate federal/bankruptcy instructions, matter reference and date needed.

Example Request

“For [subject/property] in [North Carolina county], please quote [defined UCC response and copies], the requested Register of Deeds research, and civil court research in [named court/source] for [period]. Obtain [specified documents/certifications] and identify source dates and unavailable records.”

Make the Result Assessable

Ask the deliverable to identify the exact names and property, sources and criteria used, covered period or through date, located references, supporting copies and any outstanding items. Online coverage, authentication, historical records and source response times vary. Scope, source fees and anticipated timing are confirmed for the actual matter.

Answers for Your Request

Is a deed search the same as all North Carolina land-related research?

No. The Secretary of State’s land-record guidance identifies different records at the county Register of Deeds and the Clerk of Superior Court. Specify the offices and record categories your reviewing team requires.

What should I specify for a UCC-11 or other UCC request?

Identify the exact debtor name, office, search response or certification, supporting copies and through-date requirement. Confirm the available request method and deliverable with the filing source.
