What You Receive

Your confirmed scope identifies the deliverables. Depending on the request, these can include:

Names and list scope
The supplied subject names and aliases, agreed sanctions lists and relevant identifying details used for the search.
Dated search evidence
The source, search date and available search settings or supporting evidence included in the request.
Returned results
The records or potential matches returned for the agreed list search, documented for the client’s review.
Unresolved identity questions
Potential matches and missing identifying details that require the client’s compliance or legal assessment.

Additional parties, lists, repeat searches and wider compliance analysis are not assumed to be included in a name-search request.

Define the Names and Lists to Be Searched

Request an OFAC sanctions-list search for the individuals and entities identified in your matter. Confirm the names, aliases, lists and supporting search evidence required. Additional parties or lists are separately defined scope.

Prepare Your Request

  • Exact individual or entity names; identify aliases and additional parties separately.
  • Relevant countries and identifying details already available for the matter.
  • Lists to be searched, search-date requirements and preferred documentation.
  • Matter reference, reviewing contact and date needed.

What the Search Record Should Identify

  • The supplied subject name and the agreed lists and sources.
  • The search date and any recorded search settings or identifiers.
  • Results or potential matches returned, with supporting evidence within the agreed scope.
  • Any unresolved identity questions or limits requiring the client’s review.

Clear Scope Before Work Begins

OFAC name screening supports a wider compliance review. Similar names may produce potential matches; further identifying details may be needed to assess them. A list-search result does not resolve ownership-based restrictions or other sanctions questions and is not transaction approval. The client’s legal or compliance team determines the next steps.

Answers for Your Next Request

What should I provide for an OFAC sanctions search?

Provide the exact individual or entity name, known aliases, relevant countries and identifying information already available for the matter. Confirm the lists, names, search date and documentation required. Identify additional transaction parties separately.

What does an OFAC potential match mean?

Potential matches are assessed against the subject’s identifying details, with findings provided for your compliance or legal team’s review.

Does an OFAC name search establish complete sanctions compliance?

No. It documents the agreed list search for the supplied names at a point in time. Ownership, geographic restrictions and other sanctions issues may require separate review. A no-match result is not approval of a transaction.
