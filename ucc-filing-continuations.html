Answers for Your Next Request

Can I request both state and county UCC filing work?

Yes. Identify the state filing office and any county or real-property fixture filing separately, with the property and recording requirements where applicable. The correct office and requested action are confirmed for the matter.

What do I need for an amendment, assignment, continuation or termination?

Provide the initial financing-statement number, filing office, available filing history, requested action, authorized instructions and deadline. Include approved documents or details needed to prepare the submission.

Does filing automatically include a search to reflect?

Confirm the search separately. The office’s acknowledgment and a later search of its indexed records are different deliverables. Specify the debtor name, office, search through date and copy requirements.

What is a transmitting utility UCC filing, and does it need continuations?

Purpose: A UCC financing statement provides public notice of a claimed security interest in collateral. Transmitting utility is a special debtor classification for certain transportation, communications, pipeline and utility businesses.

Where filed: A transmitting utility’s UCC financing statement is generally filed with the state’s designated central UCC filing office. Under the applicable transmitting-utility rule, that filing also serves as a fixture filing for the fixtures it identifies. Confirm the governing state’s requirements and any separate real-property recording needs.

Duration: When the debtor qualifies and the filed initial financing statement indicates transmitting-utility status, the special duration rule keeps the financing statement effective until a termination statement is filed. It does not automatically lapse after five years under that rule.

Continuations: Routine five-year continuation statements are not required for a financing statement governed by this continuing-effectiveness rule.

Termination and review: An authorized termination statement ends the filing’s effectiveness. Confirm the debtor’s classification, the initial designation, governing-state rules and any later amendments or terminations with your legal team.
