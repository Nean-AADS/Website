Services and Nationwide Support

Can AADS support nationwide due diligence?

Yes. AADS coordinates public-record research and document work across states, counties and federal courts through one point of contact. The names, jurisdictions, sources and records included are confirmed for your matter.

What records are commonly included in due diligence?

Depending on the matter, the scope can include UCC records, tax liens, judgment liens, civil litigation, bankruptcy and a certificate of good standing. The research checklist identifies the sources and jurisdictions included.

What corporate compliance services does AADS support?

AADS supports certificates of good standing, corporate record retrieval, plain and certified copies, filing submissions under authorized instructions, and registered agent coordination. The requested work and jurisdiction requirements are confirmed before proceeding.

Can AADS help obtain certificates of good standing?

Yes. Provide the exact legal entity name, issuing state, file number if known, recipient requirements and date needed. AADS coordinates the certificate of good standing or the equivalent certificate offered by that issuing office.

Preparing and Quoting Your Request

How do I prepare an individual request?

Identify the service, subject or property, jurisdictions, supporting documents and date needed. AADS confirms scope, quoted fees and anticipated timing before work begins. You can begin with one defined request.

How long does a search or document request take?

Timing depends on the service, jurisdiction, source availability and the records required. Share your deadline at the start. Anticipated timing and any available rush option are confirmed for the actual request.

What determines the quote and document copy fees?

The quote depends on the names or properties, jurisdictions, record categories, copies, certifications and any rush requirements. Confirm the service charges and applicable government or document fees in the quote before proceeding.

Packages, Records and Deliverables

Does the due diligence package cover state, county and federal sources?

Yes. The package brings together state and county UCC, tax lien and judgment lien searches, federal district and county civil litigation searches, bankruptcy research, and a certificate of good standing. The specific names, states, counties and courts are confirmed for each request.

What is included in the Real Estate Package?

The package brings together a current owner title search, recording of documents, and municipal lien searches through the responsible city, county or other local authority. Confirm the property, search depth, municipal components and recording instructions for your quote.

Does a title search include every municipal record?

Recorded land records and local departmental records are separate research paths. Identify the municipal components required, such as code, permits, utilities or assessments, and confirm the responsible departments or providers for the property.

Is a certificate of good standing the same as a certified copy?

No. A certificate of good standing addresses entity status as defined by the issuing office. A certified copy reproduces a specified filed document with that office’s certification. Provide the receiving party’s exact requirement.

Does a UCC search establish lien priority or prove that collateral is clear?

No. AADS supplies factual records and requested research from the agreed sources. The client’s legal team determines appropriate scope and the legal effect of the records, including priority and enforceability.

What is needed for an apostille or legalization request?

Identify the document, issuing authority, destination country, receiving party’s instructions and date needed. These details help establish the processing route and whether document retrieval or another certification step is needed.

Does AADS provide a legal or title opinion?

AADS provides factual research, document retrieval and filing support. The client’s legal or title professionals assess the legal effect of the records. Research scope, sources and outstanding items are identified with the agreed work.

UCC Filing and Post-Filing Requests

What is a UCC search to reflect?

The result identifies whether the specified filing appears in the office’s searchable records through the reported date, providing evidence for your legal team’s assessment.

How does a fixture filing search differ from a state UCC search?

The requests may involve different filing offices and record indexes. Identify the debtor, property, state and county and specify the sources required. A state UCC search should not be assumed to include the real-property records requested for fixture research.

Can I request UCC filings, amendments and continuations?

Yes. Identify the filing action and office, supply authorized instructions and the relevant financing statement or filing number, and state the deadline. The submission acknowledgment and any separately requested search to reflect are different deliverables.

UCC11 and Transmitting Utility Filings

What is UCC11 (UCC-11), and what should I request?

UCC11 (UCC-11) is a formal information request submitted, where accepted, to the appropriate UCC filing office for an official search of that office’s records and/or specified copies. The office conducts or processes the search through its staff or official system. The returned response and requested copies are the deliverables. Provide the exact debtor name, filing office, required scope, certification, copies and search-date requirements. The responsible office may be the Secretary of State or another agency; confirm current submission procedures.

What is a transmitting utility UCC filing, and does it need continuations?

Purpose: A UCC financing statement provides public notice of a claimed security interest in collateral. Transmitting utility is a special debtor classification for certain transportation, communications, pipeline and utility businesses.

Where filed: A transmitting utility’s UCC financing statement is generally filed with the state’s designated central UCC filing office. Under the applicable transmitting-utility rule, that filing also serves as a fixture filing for the fixtures it identifies. Confirm the governing state’s requirements and any separate real-property recording needs.

Duration: When the debtor qualifies and the filed initial financing statement indicates transmitting-utility status, the special duration rule keeps the financing statement effective until a termination statement is filed. It does not automatically lapse after five years under that rule.

Continuations: Routine five-year continuation statements are not required for a financing statement governed by this continuing-effectiveness rule.

Termination and review: An authorized termination statement ends the filing’s effectiveness. Confirm the debtor’s classification, the initial designation, governing-state rules and any later amendments or terminations with your legal team.

Property Reports, Zoning and Record Copies

Which property search depth should I request?

Specify a current-owner search, two-owner search or a defined period or chain of records. State the records, tax information and supporting copies required. AADS confirms the requested coverage; your legal or title team selects the depth appropriate to the matter.

What does a legal and vesting deed report contain?

The agreed report identifies the owner names and vesting language shown in the recorded deed, its recording reference and the property’s legal description. It is a focused record report. Request lien, mortgage, tax or broader chain research separately where needed.

What is included in an assignment-record request?

Identify the mortgage or deed of trust and request the recorded assignments and related modifications or releases within the agreed sources and period. The research can list located instruments and apparent gaps for review; it does not decide ownership of a loan or legal enforceability.

What is needed for a zoning letter, certificate of occupancy or permit-copy request?

Provide the address, parcel identifier, responsible city or county if known, exact document requested and date needed. For zoning, include the intended use and questions for the authority. For occupancy and permit records, include known permit numbers, building dates or prior addresses. Availability depends on the authority’s records.

OFAC Sanctions Searches

What should I provide for an OFAC sanctions search?

Provide the exact individual or entity name, known aliases, relevant countries and identifying information already available for the matter. Confirm the lists, names, search date and documentation required. Identify additional transaction parties separately.

What does an OFAC potential match mean?

Potential matches are assessed against the subject’s identifying details, with findings provided for your compliance or legal team’s review.

Does an OFAC name search establish complete sanctions compliance?

No. It documents the agreed list search for the supplied names at a point in time. Ownership, geographic restrictions and other sanctions issues may require separate review. A no-match result is not approval of a transaction.

Corporate Filings and Registered Agents

Can I request foreign qualification, annual reports or entity changes?

Yes. Identify the entity, formation jurisdiction, state where the filing is needed and the requested action. Provide the file number if known, authorized instructions, supporting documents and deadline. AADS confirms the filing requirements, fees and anticipated timing for that request.

What information is needed for a registered agent appointment or change?

Provide the entity’s legal name, formation state, file number if known, each state requiring an appointment, and an authorized end-client contact. Identify whether the entity is new or changing agents. Appointment acceptance, consent and filing requirements are confirmed before proceeding.

Is a registered agent appointment the same as arranging service of process?

No. An agent appointment concerns the designated recipient for notices and service of process for an entity. Arranging delivery of legal papers to another party is a separate request with different instructions and availability.

Are annual reports and ongoing monitoring included with an agent appointment?

Do not assume they are included. Confirm the appointment, notice-handling arrangements, any annual-report filing work and any separately available follow-up services in the agreed scope.

Civil, Criminal and Bankruptcy Court Research

Does a federal court search include state or county cases?

No. Federal and state or county courts maintain different records. Identify the federal districts and the state or county court sources required. A single court result should not be treated as a search of every court.

What should I provide for civil, criminal or bankruptcy court research?

Provide the exact party names and known aliases, identify each subject as an individual or entity, and specify the jurisdictions or courts, case categories, search period and date needed. For criminal searches of individuals, include the full legal name, date of birth and any known aliases for each individual, and state whether felony, misdemeanor and criminal traffic cases are required. Include case numbers where known and specify the dockets, documents, certifications and copy limits required.

Is a docket the same as the filed complaint or judgment?

No. A docket records entries and activity in a case. Request the underlying complaint, judgment, petition or other document separately where needed, identifying the case and entry when available.

What does a court search with no located cases mean?

A no-record result means no responsive case was located within the identified names, sources, criteria and period. Your report identifies the coverage and relevant access limits.
