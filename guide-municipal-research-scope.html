REQUEST-PREPARATION GUIDE · 9 NOVEMBER 2026

Start here

Treat recorded land records and local departmental responses as distinct parts of the research plan. Define the components your closing file needs before work begins.

Why separate sources matter

Your request identifies the property-specific departments, records and responses required for the matter.

For a closing checklist, the practical question is which components are included in the order and which office or provider supplies each response. A title abstract and a municipal response should not be treated as interchangeable documents.

Write down the components

Research componentWhat to specify
Recorded property recordsParcel identity, title-search depth, relevant owner or instrument references, and copies required.
Permits and codeThe requested status information, record period where relevant, and supporting case or permit records.
Utilities and assessmentsThe requested account or property-balance research and the responsible providers, when known.
Zoning documentsWhether the request needs an available online classification, an official verification letter or specified supporting documents.

Give the researcher a usable property identity

Include the street address, county, state and parcel number. Supply a legal description, owner name or prior instrument reference when available. If the property contains several parcels, identify whether the request applies to each one. An address alone may leave the scope unclear.

Plan for partial responses

Ask how separately outstanding responses will be identified. If an office has not answered, the status should remain pending; it should not be converted into a finding of no balance, violation or open permit. Confirm whether delivery is expected as a complete package or in clearly labeled stages.

Illustrative intake checklist

  • Property and parcel identifiers, including any multiple-parcel scope.
  • Requested recorded-record research and municipal components.
  • Known government offices and separate utility or service providers.
  • Supporting copies, official-letter requirements and date needed.
  • Instructions for reporting unanswered requests or unavailable records.

Does a municipal search replace a title search?

Do not assume it does. Confirm the required sources and components with the ordering team. AADS reports the agreed factual research and responses; legal interpretation and title-insurance decisions remain with the appropriate professionals.
