The Source Path Matters

PACER distinguishes a national federal case index from the records maintained by each federal court. State and county research needs its own court sources. A case listing can identify where to look; the docket and underlying documents provide different information.

Court names and responsibilities vary by state. Specify the actual sources requested and whether the search includes pending, closed or both categories of cases. Any additional case types are separately confirmed.

Criminal Searches: Federal District, State and County

For criminal searches, confirm felony, misdemeanor and criminal traffic coverage, as applicable, the search period, and whether pending cases and dispositions are required. Statewide indexes and county court searches have different coverage. Sealed, expunged, juvenile or otherwise restricted records may be unavailable. The returned work identifies the sources searched and any access limits.

Prepare Your Request

  • Exact names, entity type and additional names to search separately.
  • Requested states, counties, courts or federal districts and search period.
  • Known case numbers and any relevant docket-entry references.
  • Case categories, pending/closed requirements and the records needed.
  • For criminal searches of individuals, provide the full legal name and date of birth for each individual, plus aliases if known. Date of birth is not required for entities. Provide only information authorized for this request.
  • Plain or certified copies, page limits, matter reference and deadline.

Example Requests

“Please quote civil litigation research for [exact names] in [named state/county courts] and [federal district], covering [period]. Include the located case references and current docket information, with copies of [specified documents] up to [page limit]. Separately confirm bankruptcy scope and any inaccessible records.”

“Please quote criminal court searches for [full legal name, date of birth and known aliases for each individual] in [named federal districts], [named state courts or statewide index] and [named county court sources], covering [period]. Confirm felony, misdemeanor and criminal traffic coverage, pending-case and disposition requirements, and copies of [specified records].”

Review and Delivery

The agreed result identifies the subjects, courts or indexes, search criteria, period and located records. Returned documents are checked against the request, with unavailable items or source limits identified. Your legal team determines the appropriate scope and legal significance.

Answers for Your Request

Does a federal court search include state or county cases?

No. Federal and state or county courts maintain different records. Identify the federal districts and the state or county court sources required. A single court result should not be treated as a search of every court.

What should I provide for civil, criminal or bankruptcy court research?

Provide the exact party names and known aliases, identify each subject as an individual or entity, and specify the jurisdictions or courts, case categories, search period and date needed. For criminal searches of individuals, include the full legal name, date of birth and any known aliases for each individual, and state whether felony, misdemeanor and criminal traffic cases are required. Include case numbers where known and specify the dockets, documents, certifications and copy limits required.

Is a docket the same as the filed complaint or judgment?

No. A docket records entries and activity in a case. Request the underlying complaint, judgment, petition or other document separately where needed, identifying the case and entry when available.

What does a court search with no located cases mean?

A no-record result means no responsive case was located within the identified names, sources, criteria and period. Your report identifies the coverage and relevant access limits.
